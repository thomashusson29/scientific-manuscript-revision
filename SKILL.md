# Scientific manuscript revision

**Revise** produces the submission package and an internal assessment. **Audit** reads the existing files, reports scores and suggests corrections. Use audit mode for a scoring-only request. Keep the submitted manuscript, supplements, editorial decision and all reviewer attachments as the baseline. If several versions exist, verify which one was submitted.

Choose available agents by capability. A frontier model handles scientific judgment. A smaller model can resolve a straightforward comment end to end. Use the host's native delegation and disclose when independent grading was unavailable.

## 1. Design the rubric before drafting

A **rubric designer** using a frontier model reads the journal's official instructions for the article type and collection, the editorial decision and the original reviews. Record the sources and access dates. In audit mode, withhold the revised files and authors' replies until the rubric is frozen.

Build journal-specific criteria and weights totaling 20. One starting example is coverage (6), accuracy and evidence (5), interpretation and limitations (4), consistency and traceability (3), and clarity (2). Explain the chosen weights and distinguish journal requirements, reviewer requests and the designer's judgments. If the journal publishes few criteria, identify which defaults were retained.

Give every comment an ID. Split compound requests into separately verifiable expectations. For each, record the evidence needed for full credit, where the answer belongs, and the reason and size of a deduction. Set separate criteria for the editor's request and fidelity to the original. A **second frontier agent** challenges the rubric on the hardest cases, including justified disagreement and unavailable data. Count each underlying defect once per report. Date and freeze the rubric for that editorial round.

## 2. Protect the scientific message and resolve each request

A **fidelity reviewer** reads the original submission. Record the question, population, comparator, primary endpoint and time horizon, key estimates and uncertainty, justified methods, and limits of the conclusion. Use this record to detect drift. Correct a demonstrated error even when it appeared in the original.

A **separate frontier agent leads the revision**. Delegate routine requests by difficulty and adjudicate disputes over methods, new analyses and interpretation. Check each reviewer's premise against the manuscript, available data or primary literature.

| What the evidence shows | How to respond |
|---|---|
| The paper already answers the question. | Explain the answer in the letter, quote the passage and identify it as existing text. |
| The reader needs clarification or a correction. | Edit the smallest sufficient passage at the requested location. A request to shorten the opening paragraph applies to that paragraph. Preserve the authors' voice and useful discussion. |
| An additional analysis would answer a legitimate question. | Verify the variables, denominators and follow-up. Reproduce the result from documented code, identify post hoc analyses and place the finding where readers need it. |
| The data are unavailable or the request would support an unwarranted conclusion. | Explain what was checked, why the question remains unresolved and what this means for interpretation. Add the limitation to the paper when consequential. A supported refusal can earn full credit. |

## 3. Assemble and check the files

Write the letter in the submission language. Answer the editor first, then every reviewer in order. For each comment, give a direct response in full sentences, supporting evidence, the decision and a precise location. Quote the revised wording verbatim. For a deletion, identify the removed wording and its former location. State when the letter alone resolves a comment. Cross-reference related comments while answering each reviewer's question.

Highlight major scientific changes in yellow at phrase or sentence level. Supply clean or tracked versions when the journal requires them. Assemble the matching supplements and figures, and retain reproducible scripts for new results. Keep internal grading records and author-only code separate from files intended for submission.

Inspect native files. Verify each quotation at its stated location, compare claimed edits against the submitted version and check the highlighting. Reconcile estimates, units, denominators, time points, intervals and references across the letter, manuscript, tables, figures and supplements. Flag unverifiable statements to the author. Never fill gaps with invented data, citations, analyses or ethics approvals.

## 4. Grade independently and arbitrate

Give **fresh frontier graders** the original reports, attachments and frozen rubric first. Have them record what they will check. Then provide the revised manuscript, response letter and supporting files. Withhold drafting discussions, prior scores and expected answers. One grader may cover several reviewers but must score each separately. Grade the editor's request and fidelity to the submitted paper separately.

Show points by criterion. For every deduction, identify the passage or omission and what would earn the points back. For full credit, identify how the hardest requests were satisfied. Aim for **20/20** for every reviewer and for fidelity, with a **19/20 minimum per assessment**. These are internal judgments.

In audit mode, report the observed scores and stop. In revise mode, score the first complete package, resolve actionable deductions and regrade the actual final files, including any quotations affected by a last edit. Among candidates that meet every threshold, maximize the average of the fidelity score and the mean reviewer score, using unrounded values. Treat the editor's score separately. In an irreconcilable conflict, preserve the evidence-supported message and document the trade-off. Stop when no defensible improvement remains and report the actual scores.

Deliver the package with an internal assessment in the author's language. Identify the version graded, initial and final scores where available, outstanding deductions, and what earned credit for each reviewer: the letter alone, a manuscript edit, a supplement, an analysis or a combination.
